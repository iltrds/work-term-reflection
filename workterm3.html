Abstract / Introduction

This page will cover my third co-op work term in my computer science program at the University of Guelph. I returned to the City of Mississauga for the Summer 2026 (May-August) term as a Co-op Student in IT Support, once again with the Hardware Support Services (HSS) team. Unlike my previous term at Tech Hub, this term placed me on the technician side of HSS, working a personal ticket queue across multiple City sites. Returning to the same employer let me build directly on the foundation I established during my second work term and take on independent work far earlier than I otherwise could have.

Information about the Employer

The City of Mississauga, consolidated in 1974, is a large municipality in the Province of Ontario. Under the Region of Peel, it is responsible for multiple essential community services such as the fire department, parks maintenance, bylaw enforcement, etc. As of 2026, the City employs roughly 6000 employees across the municipality and the IT department employs around 240 to support the large employee base. Within IT, Hardware Support Services is the team responsible for the physical technology the City runs on such as laptops, desktops, mobile devices, peripherals, and printers, covering the full lifecycle from deployment through support to decommissioning.

Goals

Having already completed a work term with the City, I set goals aimed at building past what I had already learned rather than repeating it:

  • Teamwork: Contribute effectively as a collaborative team member within the Hardware Support Services team by assisting technicians, coordinating on ticket resolution, and communicating proactively about task status.
  • Problem Solving: Develop the ability to independently triage and resolve Tier 1-2 hardware support tickets, including PC imaging, peripheral setup, and basic network troubleshooting.
  • Technological Literacy: Deepen hands-on familiarity with enterprise systems used by the City of Mississauga, including Active Directory, the Help Desk ticketing system, PC Life Cycle management tools, and IT Asset Management processes.
  • Personal Organization & Time Management: Manage a personal ticket queue efficiently across multiple City sites, prioritizing tasks based on urgency and impact without supervisor direction.
  • Oral Communication: Strengthen professional communication with City staff and clients when delivering hardware support, explaining technical issues in plain language.

Job Description

I returned as a Co-op Student in IT Support on the Hardware Support Services team. HSS is responsible for the hardware that keeps the municipality running: imaging and deploying PCs, setting up and disconnecting workstations, supporting peripherals and basic network issues, and managing City hardware through its life cycle. The role is queue-driven; I self-assigned Tier 1-2 tickets out of the Help Desk system each morning and worked them across multiple City sites, escalating to senior technicians only where a ticket genuinely required it. Because I had already completed a term with HSS at Tech Hub, I was able to skip much of the ramp-up period and take on my own queue independently much earlier than in my previous term.

Conclusions

Teamwork: I consider this goal accomplished. Over the term I received consistent positive feedback both from within the IT department and externally from satisfied clients. Two examples stand out. The first was a ticket where I identified a CORS error on a website a city staff member was using; when I escalated it to the appropriate team, I was commended on the depth of my troubleshooting and told it went “above what they typically expect from a co-op student.” The second was assisting a manager with an Excel spreadsheet used for scheduling. It was full of macros written by a manager who was no longer with the city, so nobody remaining knew what they did. I did a deep dive into the scripts, got the spreadsheet working again, and then walked the manager through how to operate it himself. He emailed my supervisor about it afterwards, and I was commended by my supervisor, his manager, and the manager above him.

Problem Solving: I comfortably exceeded my 70% self-resolution target, so I consider this goal accomplished. Across the work term I resolved roughly 300 tickets, ranging from clearing hard drive space, to replacing faulty equipment, to life cycle hardware upgrades, to in-depth software and hardware diagnostics. The personal knowledge base I kept played a big role in getting me moving early in the co-op, since I could fall back on my own documented steps instead of interrupting a technician every time a familiar issue resurfaced.

Technological Literacy: Because the nature of the job was fairly independent, I became adept at the tools I was given access to, primarily ServiceNow and Active Directory. Beyond the tooling itself, I came away with a much better understanding of the structure of the city as a whole, since escalating tickets meant knowing which specialized team owned which area. Between that and the hands-on life cycle upgrade work, I consider this goal accomplished.

Personal Organization & Time Management: Not a single active ticket assigned to me went over its SLA. To manage my tickets alongside meetings, a lot of city employees needed timeslots scheduled, especially city management, I leaned heavily on Outlook Calendar, which gave me reminders and let me colour code by priority. Time management was extremely important in this role, and I got pretty good at estimating how long a given ticket should take, so my efficiency increased steadily over the work term. This goal was accomplished.

Oral Communication: As mentioned above, I received several pieces of positive client feedback, and I am noticeably more confident explaining technical processes to non-technical staff than I was at the start of the term. Surprisingly, though, it mattered less than I expected it to. Unlike my past experience at Best Buy, most people at the city were already fairly familiar with IT processes, so it was easy to get on the same page. My previous term at Tech Hub had familiarized me with city processes, but HSS put me in contact with the same people for significantly longer stretches of time. By the end of the work term, people were starting to reach out to me directly for IT support instead of submitting a ticket.

Returning to the City of Mississauga for a second term turned out to be very different from repeating one. Tech Hub was reactive and walk-up driven, where the work came to the desk and the challenge was breadth. HSS was the opposite: I owned a queue, worked it across multiple city sites, and was responsible for seeing each ticket through myself. Skipping the ramp-up period meant I could take that on almost immediately rather than spending the first month learning the systems.

Between the two terms I now have a solid picture of how a large municipal IT department actually operates day to day, from the front desk through to the technician side and the specialized teams behind both. Having staff seek me out by name by the end of the term was the clearest signal that the work I put in was worth it, and it is the part of this co-op I am proudest of.
